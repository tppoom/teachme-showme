Why compound interest snowballs

Work out what a deposit grows to, and see why the last years add more than the first.

Put ฿10,000 in an account paying 10% a year and leave it. After 10 years you might expect ฿20,000 — ten years of ฿1,000. You get ฿25,937. Where did the extra ฿5,937 come from?

Year 1 earns ฿1,000. Year 10 earns ฿2,358 — on the same 10%.

Look at the interest column. It grows every year because each year's 10% is taken of a bigger number: last year's interest is now part of the balance and earns interest too. That is all compound interest means — interest on interest.

The rule, now that you've seen it

Each year multiplies the balance by 1.10. Ten years multiply it by 1.10 ten times:

A=P× (1+r)n
where
Pwhat you put in — ฿10,000
rrate per period as a decimal — 0.10
nnumber of periods — 10 years

10,000 × 1.10¹⁰ = 25,937. The table and the formula are the same calculation; the formula just skips the rows.

The trap: the rate and the period must match

Banks quote yearly rates but often compound monthly. Then both numbers change together: r becomes the monthly rate (10% ÷ 12 ≈ 0.833%) and n counts months (120), not years. Mix them — the yearly rate with a monthly n — and you get 10,000 × 1.10¹²⁰, about ฿927 million, because you just compounded at 10% a hundred and twenty times.

You leave ฿5,000 at 4% a year for 2 years. How much interest do you earn in year 2?

฿208. Year 1 ends at ฿5,200, and 4% of ฿5,200 is ฿208 — the extra ฿8 is interest on year 1's interest.

An account pays 12% a year compounded monthly. For 3 years, what are r and n?

r = 0.01, n = 36. Rate and period must use the same unit: 12% ÷ 12 months = 1% per month, for 36 months.
